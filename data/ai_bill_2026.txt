THE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE BILL, 2026
SENATE BILLS NO. 4 OF 2026

Sponsor: Sen. Karen Nyamu.
Date of Publication: 19th February, 2026.
Date of First Reading: 2nd April, 2026.
Type of Bill: Ordinary Bill.

PURPOSE OF THE BILL
The principal object of the Bill is to regulate and govern artificial intelligence
(AI) in Kenya, ensuring ethical, transparent, and accountable use while
fostering innovation and safeguarding human rights, data protection, and
public welfare. It also establishes the Office of the Artificial Intelligence
Commissioner.

BACKGROUND
Artificial Intelligence has greatly transformed the manner in which data is
synthesised and information reproduced for various purposes. Concern has
been raised about the effects of its usage, including originality of academic and
creative work, disruption of employment, and usage by minors. The Bill
establishes a regulatory framework to leverage the benefits of AI while
mitigating negative consequences.

SCOPE
Artificial Intelligence is defined as a machine-based system or technology using
machine learning, data processing, algorithmic systems, or other methods to
operate with varying levels of autonomy and adaptiveness, inferring outputs
such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions from inputs.

OBJECT AND PURPOSE OF THE BILL
(a) provide a regulatory framework for artificial intelligence in Kenya;
(b) ensure ethical and transparent artificial intelligence use;
(c) support and foster innovation;
(d) safeguard human rights and data protection of the public;
(e) establish the Office of the Artificial Intelligence Commissioner;
(f) promote artificial intelligence literacy and provide advice to county
governments on its integration in devolved sectors; and
(g) align Kenya with international artificial intelligence standards.

THE OFFICE OF THE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE COMMISSIONER
The Bill establishes the Office of the Artificial Intelligence Commissioner, a
state office under Article 260(q) of the Constitution, independent in its
functions, with perpetual succession and capacity to sue and be sued.

Appointment: The Public Service Commission initiates recruitment, shortlists
qualified applicants, conducts interviews, and nominates three qualified
applicants in order of merit to the President, who nominates one for
Parliament's approval.

Qualifications: a masters degree in AI, computer science, ICT, engineering,
data science, law, ethics or related field; at least ten years' experience in a
relevant field (AI governance, data protection, technology policy, regulatory
oversight, ethics, human rights, risk management); at least ten years managing
public or private institutions; and compliance with Chapter Six of the
Constitution.

Term: five years, eligible for one further five-year term.

Removal grounds: death, resignation, conviction with imprisonment exceeding
six months, serious constitutional violation, gross misconduct, incapacity,
incompetence, or bankruptcy — following a complaint to the Public Service
Commission and an opportunity to respond.

FUNCTIONS OF THE OFFICE
Overseeing implementation and enforcement of the Act; conducting risk
assessments of AI systems; performing conformity audits and post-market
surveillance; assessing high-risk AI systems for compliance; developing
policies, guidelines and standards on AI governance, ethics and safety;
promoting responsible development and deployment of AI; establishing
regulatory sandboxes for safe innovation and testing; receiving and
investigating AI-related complaints; maintaining a public register of high-risk
AI systems (including those used by county governments); advising national
and county governments on AI policy integration; promoting AI literacy and
capacity building; and conducting research and monitoring trends.

ADMINISTRATIVE POWERS
The Office may inspect premises, systems and records; require information
from AI providers; issue enforcement notices or impose fines; summon persons
to give evidence; collaborate with international bodies; establish an appeals
mechanism; and delegate powers to authorised officers.

THE ADVISORY COMMITTEE ON ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE
A multi-stakeholder body advising the Commissioner, comprising: the
Commissioner (Chairperson); a Ministry of ICT representative; a
representative of the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner; a NACOSTI
representative; two AI ethics/human rights experts appointed by the Cabinet
Secretary (three-year renewable term); two persons of each gender nominated
by the Council of Governors; and one civil society representative with
technology or human rights expertise. Meets at least four times a year.

RISK CLASSIFICATION OF AI SYSTEMS
The Commissioner classifies AI systems by risk to health, safety, fundamental
rights, environment or societal welfare into four categories:
(a) unacceptable risk — systems posing severe threats;
(b) high risk — systems used in critical sectors including healthcare, education,
agriculture, finance, security, employment or public administration;
(c) limited risk — systems with moderate risks; and
(d) minimal risk — systems with negligible risks.

OBLIGATIONS FOR HIGH-RISK AI SYSTEMS
Providers and deployers must: conduct risk and human rights impact
assessments before deployment and implement mitigation measures; ensure
transparency, traceability and explainability of decision-making; keep
datasets and system documentation; comply with the Data Protection Act;
ensure robustness and cybersecurity; and obtain consent when using a
person's likeness or voice.

TRANSPARENCY MEASURES
AI providers and deployers must disclose the nature and purpose of the
system, how much automation is involved, and measures taken to mitigate
bias. Users must ensure human intervention rights where automated
decisions affect individuals.

WORKFORCE IMPACT OBLIGATIONS
Where AI impacts employment, providers must conduct workforce impact
assessments (including potential job displacement) and implement mitigation
measures including reskilling programmes with national and county
government agencies. The Commissioner shall develop guidelines on
workforce transition, vocational training partnerships, and incentives for
job-creating AI adoption.

OFFENCES AND PENALTIES
A person commits an offence for: deploying an unacceptable-risk AI system;
deploying a high-risk system without required risk assessment/mitigation;
failing disclosure/transparency obligations; participating in a regulatory
sandbox without adhering to conditions; failing to conduct a workforce impact
assessment; contravening ethical guidelines causing bias, discrimination or
harm; misusing AI in the public sector to the prejudice of public rights;
obstructing the Office; or generating/distributing AI content (including
synthetic media) using a person's image, voice or likeness without consent
where it causes harm, misinformation, defamation or privacy infringement.

Penalties: for the more serious offences, a fine not exceeding five million
shillings or imprisonment not exceeding two years, or both. For lesser
offences, a fine not exceeding one million shillings or imprisonment not
exceeding six months, or both. Where committed by a body corporate, any
director or officer with knowledge who failed to exercise due diligence is also
guilty.

REGULATIONS
The Cabinet Secretary, in consultation with the Commissioner, may make
regulations on: risk classification criteria; risk assessment processes; regulatory
sandbox conditions; ethical guideline content and review; data governance
frameworks; enforcement and appeals procedures; public participation and AI
literacy programmes; fees and administrative procedures; consent and
labelling of AI-generated content; and exceptions for legitimate image
manipulation uses.

PERIODIC REVIEW
Every three years, the Cabinet Secretary must review the Act, consult
stakeholders, assess new risks, and report to Parliament with recommended
amendments, within six months of completing the review.
